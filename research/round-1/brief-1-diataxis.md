# Agent 1: Diátaxis for Cartograph

Read COMMON.md in this folder first.

Study Diátaxis in depth (start-here, the map, the compass, each of the
four kinds, the pages on writing tutorials and how-to guides, reference
and explanation, and anything on tone, language and complex hierarchies).
Then write **diataxis-guide.md**: Cartograph's documentation guide, the
common input for the rest of the work. It must contain:

1. **The framework in brief**, in your own words: the two axes, the four
   kinds, the compass questions, with a source link for each point.
2. **Cartograph's users on the map.** For each kind of user (a
   non-technical project officer meeting Cartograph for the first time;
   the same person on their tenth project; a sponsor or approver; a PMO
   or strategy analyst; a data person who specifies indicators and data
   sources; an administrator who deploys and configures it; a developer
   or distribution builder; an AI agent over MCP), list their needs in
   each quadrant: what they would study, what tasks they do, what they
   look up, what they want to understand. Be concrete to Cartograph's
   kinds and screens (read the repositories).
3. **The map as a UX lens.** Diátaxis distinguishes learning from doing.
   Say how that bears on the interface: which screens serve study and
   which serve work, what a "tutorial" experience in the product is
   (the onboarding walk), what a "how-to" path is (a task walk), what
   "reference" is in the product (sheets, the workspace, the charter),
   and what "explanation" is (guidance, help, why a check exists). Point
   out where today's screens mix modes (read src/copy.ts and the routes).
4. **The documentation's structure**: a proposed table of contents in the
   four kinds, with a one-line purpose for each page, and how the
   landing page should orient a newcomer.
5. **Tone and style rules** with short do and don't examples in
   Cartograph's own vocabulary, following Diátaxis's language guidance
   (conversational, second person, the "we" of a tutorial, the
   imperative of a how-to, the neutral description of reference, the
   discursive voice of explanation).
