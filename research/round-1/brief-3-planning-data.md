# Agent 3: planning ahead without doing all the planning, and data capture specifications

Read COMMON.md in this folder first, and Diátaxis.

Part A: **thinking ahead, not planning everything.** Research how trusted
sources treat the planning that belongs at initiation versus later:
progressive elaboration and rolling wave planning (PMI), stage
boundaries and planning horizons (PRINCE2), milestone planning, the
pre-mortem (Gary Klein, HBR), assumptions and RAID logs, dependency
mapping, benefits management (APM, MSP / Managing Successful
Programmes), the definition of ready, and estimation at low maturity
(ranges, cones of uncertainty). Say what a charter-stage "plan ahead"
should ask (enough to decide and to start), what it should leave to the
team later, and how a tool can prompt the thinking without becoming a
scheduling tool.

Part B: **data capture specifications.** Research what a good
specification of an indicator and its data contains: USAID Performance
Indicator Reference Sheets (PIRS), UN SDG indicator metadata, the World
Bank and OECD guidance on results indicators, SMART and CREAM criteria,
data quality dimensions (DAMA DMBOK, ISO 8000 or ISO/IEC 25012), data
dictionaries and data contracts, baselines and targets, disaggregation,
reporting frequency, responsibility and the source of each figure.

For both parts, compare with Cartograph: read the KPI, DataSource,
ReportingCycle, Unit and Project schemas and guidance in
cartograph-engine/contract, and the indicator builder in cartograph-ui.
Say what is essential for a non-technical person to give, what a power
user may add, and what is missing.
