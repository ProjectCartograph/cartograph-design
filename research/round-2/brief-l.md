# Agent L: large projects with sub-projects

Research how a large project is broken into parts and governed, from:
PMI (subprojects and components in the PMBOK Guide and The Standard for
Project Management; the Practice Standard for Work Breakdown Structures);
ISO 21502:2020 on projects, programmes and their relationship; PRINCE2
(stages, work packages, and when a project should be a programme
instead); APM Body of Knowledge; the Infrastructure and Projects
Authority's guidance on major projects (and the Project Initiation
Routemap); research on megaprojects (Flyvbjerg's Oxford research, the
iron law of megaprojects, reference class forecasting, where you can
reach peer-reviewed or official text); and what the sources say about
**when a project has become a programme** (outputs versus outcomes,
one deliverable versus many, the signs). Cover how sub-projects inherit
or own their objectives, budgets, risks, schedules and governance, how
the parent tracks them, and how dependencies between them are handled.

Write large-projects.md with: (1) how a large project is decomposed and
governed, by source, with a table; (2) what a sub-project needs of its
own and what it inherits; (3) project versus programme: the tests the
sources give; (4) the evidence on large projects' risks and what a tool
can do about them (ranges, reference classes, staging); (5) Cartograph
against it, reading the Project schema's components and dependencies,
the critical path, the guidance and TAXONOMY decisions on components;
(6) the Diátaxis needs of a person running a large project and of a
sub-project lead.
