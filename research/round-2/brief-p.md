# Agent P: programmes, and long horizons

Research what defining a programme involves, from: AXELOS/PeopleCert
Managing Successful Programmes (MSP 5th edition: vision, blueprint or
target operating model, benefits map and profiles, tranches, programme
mandate and brief, the programme definition document); PMI's Standard
for Program Management (4th edition: benefits management, program life
cycle, program roadmap, governance); APM Body of Knowledge on programmes;
GovS 002 and the Infrastructure and Projects Authority (programme
business cases, the Government Major Projects Portfolio, gateway
reviews); the HM Treasury Green Book and its business case guidance for
programmes (programme business cases, the five case model at programme
level); and theory of change for long programmes. Focus on **long time
horizons**: tranches and roadmaps, benefits that fall years after the
work, re-baselining, keeping a vision steady while projects come and go,
reviewing whether the programme still makes sense.

Write programmes.md: (1) what a programme definition holds, by source,
with an element-by-source table; (2) what is essential, what common,
what later; (3) how a long horizon changes what is asked and how often
it is revisited; (4) what differs from a project charter (round one);
(5) Cartograph against it: read the Programme schema, guidance, flow,
TAXONOMY decisions and the programme screens; what it asks, what it
lacks, what it asks that it should not; (6) the Diátaxis needs of a
programme manager and a programme sponsor (study, work, look up,
understand).
