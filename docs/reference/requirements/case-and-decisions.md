# The case and the decisions

The justification for a piece of work, how it grows, and the decisions taken on it.

Each requirement is a statement that can be checked. Its status starts Proposed and becomes Approved, Changed or Dropped in review ([how to review a requirement](../../how-to/review-a-requirement.md)). Sources are in [the bibliography](../bibliography.md).

### D1 A case that grows

The justification (the need, options, benefits, cost, risk, how it will be delivered) is one record, revised before each decision and held at several levels of completeness.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Staged business cases, design stages and funding cycles all grow one case and decide on it at each step.
- **Accepted when:**
  - The case has one record per piece of work, with a version per revision.
  - Each part of the case shows how complete it is.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, public sector elsewhere, development finance, construction, project standards
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 4.13 to 4.25; [NZT-BBC](../bibliography.md); [WB-CYCLE](../bibliography.md); [RIBA](../bibliography.md); [PRINCE2](../bibliography.md) project brief, business case
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D2 Five questions at any scale

The same five questions can be asked at any scale: is there a need, is it good value, is it viable to buy or build, is it affordable, and can it be delivered.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** The five case model asks these whatever the size; only the depth changes.
- **Accepted when:**
  - The parts of the case are grouped under the five questions.
  - A one-page brief and a full business case draw on the same parts.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, public sector elsewhere
- **Evidence:** [HMT-GREEN](../bibliography.md) five case model; [NZT-BBC](../bibliography.md)
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D3 If nothing new is done

The record says what is expected if nothing new is done (the organisation carrying on as it is, with its own costs, benefits and risks), or says plainly that this is not known.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Appraisal and evaluation measure against what would happen anyway, and carrying on as now is not free.
- **Accepted when:**
  - The case holds a statement of what happens if nothing new is done, or a stated unknown.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, evaluation, impact investment
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 7.10 to 7.12; [OECD-CRITERIA](../bibliography.md) efficiency; [IMPACT-FRONTIERS](../bibliography.md)
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D4 Options considered

The options considered, including carrying on as now and a minimum option, and why one was preferred, can be recorded before commitment; a profile for public or funded investment requires them.

- **Priority:** Should
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Public and funded investment must compare a shortlist against carrying on as now; a hobbyist need not.
- **Accepted when:**
  - The case can hold options, each with why it was kept or set aside.
  - Where the profile requires options, a decision to commit is not offered without them.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, public sector elsewhere
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 8.23, 8.45; [NZT-BBC](../bibliography.md)
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D5 A decision is a record

A decision is a record of who decided, in what role, when, the outcome (approve, approve with conditions, change the scope, try another option, pilot, defer or stop), the reasons, and the version of the case it rested on.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Every life cycle studied turns on recorded decisions; the outcomes follow the Treasury's list, with approval under conditions added as boards use it.
- **Accepted when:**
  - A decision names the version of the case it rested on.
  - A recorded decision cannot be edited; a later decision supersedes it.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, project standards
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 4.14, 5.14; [GOVS002](../bibliography.md) 6.3; [NISTA-GATES](../bibliography.md); [PMI-PORTFOLIO](../bibliography.md) 5.4
- **Verified by:** an automated test in the build

### D6 Conditions on a decision

Each condition on a decision has an owner, a due point and what it holds back, and the record shows when it is met.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Funding and approvals are often given with conditions; without an owner and a due point, a condition is not followed up.
- **Accepted when:**
  - Each condition has an owner, a due point and what it holds back.
  - Unmet conditions past their due point are shown on the work.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 5.14, 10.24
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D7 Stopping is ordinary

Stopping, pausing and cancelling are ordinary outcomes with a reason, and the work stays readable afterwards.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** The standards treat stopping or resetting as a legitimate decision, and stopped work holds lessons.
- **Accepted when:**
  - A stopped piece of work keeps every part and can still be read.
  - Stopping records a reason.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, project standards
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md) 3.3, 4.14; [GOVS002](../bibliography.md) 6.3; [PMI-PORTFOLIO](../bibliography.md) 5.4
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D8 An approved plan, and changes to it

The approved plan (scope, cost, dates, benefits) is kept as a named version; the current plan is compared with it; a change to the approved plan is its own recorded decision with its reason and authority, apart from correcting a mistake.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Change control and later reviews need the approved plan and its changes kept apart from everyday edits.
- **Accepted when:**
  - The approved plan is a named version of the record.
  - Changing the approved plan requires a decision; correcting a mistake does not.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector
- **Evidence:** [GOVS002](../bibliography.md) 7.7; [NAO-PROGRAMMES](../bibliography.md); [NISTA-GATES](../bibliography.md) Gate 5
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D9 The chain upward

A piece of work draws on the level above (the strategy, a programme's case) and can show the chain upward.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Cases nest: much of a project's strategic case is its programme's.
- **Accepted when:**
  - A view of a piece of work can show the goals and parent work it serves, to the top.
- **Holds across:** UK public sector, public sector elsewhere
- **Evidence:** [HMT-CASES](../bibliography.md); [TBS-RESULTS](../bibliography.md)
- **Verified by:** comparing a rendered view with the record it came from

### D10 Portfolio decisions

A portfolio's decision to fund, hold, stop or move money between its members is a decision record, with the criteria and the objective each serves when the portfolio uses them.

- **Priority:** Must
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Authorising, prioritising, stopping and reallocating are what a portfolio decides.
- **Accepted when:**
  - A portfolio records a decision on each member.
  - Where criteria are used, each decision shows the scores behind it.
- **Holds across:** project standards, UK public sector, software
- **Evidence:** [PMI-PORTFOLIO](../bibliography.md) 5.4; [TEAL](../bibliography.md) chapter 12; [SAFE](../bibliography.md) lean portfolio management
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples

### D11 Still relevant, and not duplicated

At each review a piece of work can be asked whether its objectives are still relevant as circumstances change, and whether other work supports, undermines or duplicates it.

- **Priority:** Should
- **Status:** Proposed
- **Why:** Evaluators ask about relevance and coherence, and benefits guidance asks for objectives to be rechecked over time.
- **Accepted when:**
  - A review can record whether the objectives remain relevant.
  - A review can name other work that overlaps.
- **Holds across:** evaluation, UK public sector
- **Evidence:** [OECD-CRITERIA](../bibliography.md) relevance, coherence; [IPA-BENEFITS](../bibliography.md) 7.7
- **Verified by:** inspection of the model's schemas and their examples
