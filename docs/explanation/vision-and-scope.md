# Vision and scope

Cartograph helps people write down what they have decided to do and why, and steer it. This page explains who it is for, what they need from it, and where its edges are. Every requirement in [the catalogue](../reference/requirements/README.md) serves one of the needs below.

## The people it serves

Cartograph is for anyone who must justify and steer work: a hobbyist's group, a cooperative, a charity, a research team, a company, a public body, a national government. Some of them are technical and many are not. Some define one piece of work; others define programmes over long horizons, portfolios of investment, or projects large enough to have parts of their own.

They share one need, which they state as:

> We need to write down what we have decided to do and why, so that others can see it, challenge it, fund it or carry it on, and so that we can tell whether it is working and change it when it is not.

## What they need

| Need | Mode of use | Who has it most |
|---|---|---|
| Say what we want to change and why it matters, in plain language, without learning a method first | Learning | Everyone, above all non-technical people |
| See where we are and what to do next, without a wall of controls | Doing | Everyone |
| Say how the work will bring the change about, and what we are assuming | Doing, understanding | Sponsors, managers, funders |
| Say how we will know it worked, and record what we find | Doing | Managers, analysts |
| Show it to a board, funder, minister or partner in a form they already accept | Doing | Officers, sponsors |
| Find out what a term means or what a field expects | Looking up | Everyone |
| Understand why the tool asks what it asks, and how the parts connect | Understanding | Managers, portfolio offices, analysts |
| Change our minds, and see what changed and who agreed | Doing | Everyone in a team |
| Have the decisions outlive us, the tool and the organisation | Doing | Administrators, sponsors, public bodies |
| Load, query and script in bulk, and connect agents, without the walk in the way | Doing, looking up | Analysts, developers, agents |
| Be fair to the people the work affects, including those who never read it | Understanding | Charities, public bodies, impact funders |

The modes are Diataxis's: a person moves between learning, doing, looking up and understanding, and the same person may be a newcomer one day and an expert the next ([DIATAXIS](../reference/bibliography.md)). The design serves each mode in its own way rather than averaging them.

## What a good result looks like

A person who has never used Cartograph says what they want to change, one question at a time and in plain language, and ends with a record their board, funder or team accepts. They always know the next step. Anyone who wants the full workspace finds it one step away. The same record serves a hobbyist and a national government, each in its own taxonomy and life cycle, and outlives the tool.

These are measured, not asserted: by sessions with people traced, with their consent, as they work, and by an audit of what each screen shows ([P2](../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p2-one-decision-per-screen), [K7](../reference/requirements/platform.md#k7-measured-on-peoples-use-by-consent)).

## In scope

- Defining a piece of work at any scale: its purpose, the change it seeks, how that change is expected to happen, its options, measures, money, risks and people.
- The decisions taken on it through its life, the reviews that inform them, and the plan they approve.
- How progress and benefits are known, during the work and after it ends.
- Groups of work: programmes, portfolios and work with parts.
- Showing the record to each audience in the form it accepts, and publishing it in open standards.
- Working alone or together, with people and agents.

## Out of scope

Cartograph holds the results of these and links to the tools that do them; it does not do them itself ([X6](../reference/requirements/domains-and-configuration.md#x6-hold-results-link-to-tools)):

- Task management, backlogs and detailed schedules.
- Accounting, payments and procurement.
- Economic appraisal models and statistical analysis.
- Building information models and engineering design.
- Data warehousing and dashboards over operational data.
