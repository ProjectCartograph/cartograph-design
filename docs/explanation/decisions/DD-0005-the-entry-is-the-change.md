# DD-0005 The entry is the change

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Requirements:** [P1](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p1-start-from-the-change-with-no-choice-first), [P2](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p2-one-decision-per-screen), [E1](../../reference/requirements/change-and-evaluation.md#e1-how-the-change-is-expected-to-happen), [R2](../../reference/requirements/shape-of-the-record.md#r2-the-kind-is-a-label-given-after), [P4](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p4-the-persons-vocabulary), [P11](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p11-a-programme-set-out-in-order)

## Context

People in the field gave up at the first screen, where they were asked to choose a kind of work, and finished only a guided walk ([FIELD](../../reference/bibliography.md)). The frameworks for justifying work and the methods for planning change all begin in the same place. The Treasury's business case starts with the strategic case, which sets out why a proposal is needed ([HMT-CASES](../../reference/bibliography.md) 2.4); New Zealand's programme case starts with problem statements and what happens if nothing changes ([NZT-PBC](../../reference/bibliography.md) 5.3 to 5.5); the logical framework analyses the problem, then the objectives, then the strategy ([EC-PCM](../../reference/bibliography.md) 3.2); a theory of change works backwards from the long-term goal to what it needs ([ACTK](../../reference/bibliography.md)); and digital services start from what their users need ([GDS-MANUAL](../../reference/bibliography.md) user needs). Design research adds that a person who is asked to sort work into a tool's categories before describing it misreads the tool, while one who meets terms they already use does not ([NNG](../../reference/bibliography.md) mental models). The order is the same everywhere: what is wrong, what should be different and for whom, how it will come about, how we will know, and only then who decides and what it costs. A first walk could be organised around any of three things.

| Entry | Makes easy | Makes hard |
|---|---|---|
| The decision and its case | Approval, audit, boards | Steering over time; it reads as form filling |
| The change wanted | The why and the how-will-we-know, in plain language | Money and authority need their own place |
| The unit of work | Money, dates, exchange | Output-shaped; brings back classifying first |

## Decision

The first walk asks for the **change**: what is wrong, what should be different, for whom, how it is expected to come about, and how we will know. The piece of work, its label and its case are built from the answers; the decision and the documents that ask for it follow from the record ([DD-0010](DD-0010-views-are-renderings.md)).

The entry rests on the sources above, which agree on where definition starts, and not on a test with people: what a sound case starts from is a matter of the expertise the frameworks hold. Sessions with people check whether the walk is easy to use, not what it asks first.

## Consequences

- Home offers to start from a change, and to pick up where the person left off.
- The label is suggested after the first answers, never asked first.
- The walk is still tested with people for ease of use: whether they find the next step, leave and come back, and finish unaided ([P2](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p2-one-decision-per-screen), [P3](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p3-leave-and-come-back), [P7](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p7-finishable-unaided-by-anyone)).
- The programme walk continues from the same answers in the order of [P11](../../reference/requirements/people-and-first-use.md#p11-a-programme-set-out-in-order).
